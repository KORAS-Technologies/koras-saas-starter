# High contrast

**Where:** your own preferences, at `/dashboard/preferences`, under **Accessibility**.

## What it does

Turn **High contrast** on and the signed-in product redraws itself at the strongest
contrast it can: black text on a white page in the light appearance, white on black in
the dark one. It applies everywhere you are signed in — the header, the navigation, the
page and the panels on it.

Because surfaces stop being distinguished by a faint tint, panels and the navigation are
separated by a clear border instead.

## What it does not change

- **Your appearance choice.** High contrast works *within* light or dark; it does not pick
  one for you. Switching between light and dark still does what it always did.
- **Your organisation's brand colour.** Buttons and the focus outline keep your
  organisation's colour. Only the parts that decide whether text is readable — the page,
  the panels, the text and the borders — change.
- **Anyone else's screen.** This is your preference, on your account. A colleague signed
  in to the same organisation is unaffected.
- **Your computer's own high-contrast mode.** If you have one turned on in Windows or
  macOS, that continues to take precedence. The two do not fight, and you do not need to
  turn this on to use that.

## Turning it on

1. Go to **Preferences**.
2. Under **Accessibility**, switch **High contrast** on.
3. Save.

The change appears the next time a page loads — move to another page, or reload, and the
product is redrawn. Turning it off returns the product to exactly how it looked before.

## If your organisation has set a default

An administrator can set a default for everyone in the organisation. Your own choice
always wins over that default: if you turn high contrast on, you get it, whatever the
organisation's default is, and if you turn it off you get it off.

## What is not covered yet

**Text size** and **Reduce motion** appear beside this setting and do not work yet. They
are recorded as outstanding; turning them on today changes nothing. High contrast is the
first of the three to be honoured.
