# KORAS Control Plane Profile

This repository is the **KORAS Control Plane** — the authority that administers
the KORAS platform itself. Generated by
`create-koras-app --profile control-plane`; confirm with `.koras/project.yaml`,
where `project.profile` reads `control-plane`.

Its users are KORAS staff operating the platform, plus customer administrators
in the account portal. It is **not** a customer-facing SaaS product, and it must
bootstrap without a pre-existing Control Plane.

## Domains

Dashboard · Organizations · Products · Tenants · Plans · Subscriptions ·
Entitlements · Domains · Branding · Identity · Enterprise SSO · AI · Storage ·
Infrastructure · Provisioning · Reconciliation · Audit · Platform Health

Each is a first-class area of the platform admin surface. A change that adds
state to one of these owns its list view, its detail view, its audit events and
its health signal — not just its table.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **Never expose infrastructure secrets.** Provider tokens, service-role keys,
   Terraform state, machine credentials and connection strings are referenced,
   never rendered. The admin UI shows *that* a credential is configured, its
   provider and its rotation age — never its value, never a partial value long
   enough to be useful. Nothing privileged reaches a `NEXT_PUBLIC_*` variable.
2. **Never weaken `platform_admin` authorization.** Not to unblock local
   development, not behind an environment check, not with a bypass flag. If a
   flow is hard to test, fix the test fixture, not the authorization.
3. **Navigation visibility is never authorization.** Hiding a menu entry, a tab
   or a button is presentation. Every admin action is authorized server-side, on
   the request, every time — including actions only reachable from a hidden
   route.
4. **Enforce admin actions server-side.** A destructive operation validates the
   principal, the permission, the target's current state and the operation's
   preconditions in the handler. The client's job is to ask.
5. **Destructive actions are deliberate and reversible where possible.**
   De-provisioning, tenant deletion, plan removal, domain release and
   credential rotation each require explicit confirmation naming the target,
   an audit event written before the effect, and a stated blast radius. Prefer
   soft-delete and scheduled teardown over immediate irreversible destruction.
6. **Everything privileged is audited.** Who, what, which target, when, from
   where, and the outcome — including denials. Audit events carry no secrets.
7. **The Control Plane never registers itself as a product.** It has no tenant
   tables of its own and no customer billing lifecycle.

## Operational surface

- **Information density over whitespace.** These are operator consoles. Tables
  with sortable, filterable columns beat card grids; a row should carry
  identity, state, environment, owner and last-changed without a hover.
- **State must be legible at a glance.** Provisioning, reconciliation and health
  each have an explicit status vocabulary — pending, running, drifted, failed,
  healthy — rendered consistently, with a timestamp and a route to the
  underlying record.
- **Show provenance.** Where a value came from (Terraform, Doppler, a manual
  override, a reconciliation run) is part of the value.
- **Long-running work is observable.** Provisioning and reconciliation runs
  expose progress, current step, elapsed time and a failure reason that names
  the resource. Never a spinner with no end state.
- **Drift is surfaced, not silently corrected.** Reconciliation reports what
  diverged and what it would do before it does it.

## Accessibility and UX

Dense does not mean inaccessible. Target WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard operability
throughout, visible focus, real table semantics, labelled controls, and error
messages that name the resource and the failure. Operators work these screens
all day and often by keyboard.

## Testing

Alongside `koras-testing`, a Control Plane change is not complete until
authorization is tested negatively: a principal *without* `platform_admin` must
be shown to be unable to reach the operation through the surface being changed,
including by direct request to the handler. Destructive operations are tested
for their confirmation path and their audit record.

## What does not belong here

Customer-facing product features, tenant-owned application data, per-tenant
business logic and product billing UX belong in a KORAS product repository
(`--profile product`). The Control Plane defines plans, entitlements and
provisioning; products consume them.
